Recommendation with multi-armed bandits

Learning which video to show from logged feedback alone, and measuring it off-policy.

github.com/hajar6883/bandits-online-allocation

The constraint

A recommender has to choose what to show before it knows what anybody likes. Every slot spent on an unknown video is a slot not spent on one that already works, and every slot spent on the safe choice is a video you never learn about. That is the explore/exploit trade-off, and it is the whole problem.

The harder part is evaluation. You cannot A/B test a policy against a dataset that has already been collected: if your policy wants to show video 42 to a user who never saw it, there is no reward to give it. So the measurement has to work off logged decisions only.

How it works

  KuaiRec interaction log        4.7M rows
        |
        v
   prepare     join users + items, sub-sample, drop leakage columns
        |
        v
   featurise   reward = 1 if watch_ratio > 0.8
                arm = video (85), context = 26 user features
        |
        v
   evaluate    5 policies x 500 rounds x 250 trials, off-policy replay
        |
        v
   results  ·  reward curves  ·  arm frequencies

Five policies, split by whether they look at who the user is:

  • Non-contextual — epsilon-greedy, UCB1, Thompson Sampling. Video 42 is good or bad, full stop.
  • Contextual — LinUCB, Linear Thompson Sampling. Video 42 may suit this user and not the next.

Evaluation is off-policy replay: the learner picks an arm, and a logged reward for that arm is read back from the interaction data. No simulator, no model inventing counterfactuals.

The whole thing runs as a Dataiku DSS Flow: three Python recipes, a scenario that force-rebuilds the chain from raw data, and a dashboard comparing the policies.

Evidence

  • Over 500 rounds x 250 trials on 85 arms: LinUCB 0.918 against 0.678 to 0.778 for the non-contextual policies. The logged baseline rate is 0.508.
  • Context is worth roughly 0.14 mean reward over the best non-contextual policy, on real user features rather than synthetic ones.
  • The port to Dataiku is verified, not assumed: the policy files are byte-identical to the originals, the rebuilt reward and context structures match exactly (85 arms, 26 dimensions, 0 differing), and with a shared seed the reward matrices are element-wise identical.
  • One caveat kept in the open: LinUCB always evaluates context index 0, so it exploits a fixed context per arm where LinTS samples one. Part of its margin is structural.

Extractive meeting summarisation

Deciding which lines of a transcribed meeting belong in the record.

github.com/hajar6883/DiscGraph_Summarization

The constraint

Meeting minutes are a filtering problem before they are a writing problem. Given an automatically transcribed meeting, decide for every utterance whether it belongs in the summary. One binary label per line, so this is classification, not generation.

Two things make it awkward. The text is machine-transcribed and messy: misspellings, half-finished sentences, artefact tokens like <vocalsound>. And the labels are lopsided, 59,331 drop against 13,292 keep, so always answering "drop" already scores 82% accuracy while producing an empty summary. Accuracy is the wrong metric; F1 on the positive class is the right one.

How it works

  ASR transcript  +  discourse graph (16 relation types)
        |
        v
   frozen encoders   all-mpnet-base-v2 · bert-base-uncased
                     nothing fine-tuned
        |
        +--> LSTM    own 3k vocabulary, sequence over utterances
        |
        +--> HGAT    hierarchical GATv2 over the discourse graph
        |
        v
   keep / drop  per utterance

The interesting part was a negative result. A cleaning pipeline was written first: lowercasing, contraction expansion, removing ASR artefacts, stopwords, onomatopoeia, a minimum word length filter. Rather than assume it helped, each step was measured.

Every step except contraction expansion made F1 worse, so the pipeline was dropped. On transcribed speech, cleaning removed more signal than noise. The file is still in the repo with its body commented out, as a record of what was tried.

Evidence

  • LSTM F1 0.596 against HGAT 0.533. The smaller sequence model beat the graph model, even though the HGAT is larger and uses the discourse structure the task supposedly needs.
  • Against a trivial all-positive baseline at 0.31, on 7,263 validation utterances.
  • Class imbalance handled two ways so the comparison was fair: negatives downsampled to 25,000 for the LSTM, class-balanced cross-entropy for the HGAT.
  • The dropped cleaning pipeline is the result worth keeping: it was measured step by step instead of assumed, and removed because the measurement said so.
